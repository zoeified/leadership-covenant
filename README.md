# Leadership Covenant

A leadership covenant is a written set of promises a leader makes to the people they lead. This repository holds an example covenant, shared as a starting point for the leaders who attended my talk and for anyone who wants to write their own.

A job description usually points one direction; it tells the team what the leader expects of them. This covenant points the other way. It puts the leader's own commitments in writing, signs them, and hands the team something they can hold the leader to.

## What a leadership covenant does

A covenant turns the way you intend to lead into something visible and reviewable. It names the commitments you are willing to be measured against, and it invites the people you lead to hold you to them. When you read it on a regular schedule, it keeps your day-to-day leadership honest against the standard you set for yourself.

The example here is written from a director to a team, though the structure adapts to other leaders and teams.

## What you'll find in this repository

- `Leadership Covenant -- 2026.06.04.docx`: the editable example covenant, ready to adapt.
- `README.md`: this file.

The covenant document has three parts:

- A short preamble that frames the leader's intent to give an account for their leadership.
- Nine first-person commitments covering trust, knowing how decisions affect each person, honest listening, acting in their best interest, shared decision-making, backing them up under pressure, keeping promises, modeling the values, and handling conflict directly.
- A signature and date block, with blanks for the team name and the individual's name.

## How to adapt the covenant for your team

1. Download the `.docx` and fill in the blanks for your team and for the people you lead.
2. Read each commitment and rewrite it in your own words, so it reflects how you actually intend to lead.
3. Add or remove commitments to fit your context; treat the nine here as a starting point you can adjust.
4. Sign and date it, then share it with your team.

The value comes from the commitments being true and your team knowing you mean them. A covenant you sign and set aside does little; one you review and live by changes how your team experiences your leadership.

## Reviewing the covenant over time

Set a regular rhythm to reread the covenant, quarterly works well, and ask whether your leadership has matched it. Invite the people you lead to tell you where it has and where it hasn't. That feedback is the point.
